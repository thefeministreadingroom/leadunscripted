Theatre-based training

Don’t just talk about it. Rehearse it.

Applied theatre uses role play, improvisation, theatre games and storytelling to explore real situations at work. Not for performance, but for real learning. People don’t discuss the difficult conversation. They step into it, try it, and try again until it works.

What it is

Learning you step into

Instead of a slide about conflict, the room experiences the conflict, the decision and the difficult conversation, in real time and in a safe space. That is what makes the learning stick.

Role play

Real situations from your workplace, played out and replayed.

Improvisation

Thinking on your feet, listening and responding in the moment.

Theatre games

Short, playful exercises that build trust, presence and energy.

Storytelling

Turning experience into stories people remember and act on.

Why it sticks

Practice, not performance

  • You do it, not just discuss it. Trying a new approach once, in the room, makes it far easier to use at work on Monday.
  • Safe to get it wrong. A scene can be paused, rewound and replayed, so mistakes become the lesson.
  • You feel it, so you remember it. Stepping into someone else’s shoes builds empathy that a slide cannot.
  • Rehearsed until it feels natural. Each replay gets closer to how you want to sound under pressure.

How a session runs

Five moves, every time

  1. 1

    Warm up

    Theatre games build trust, so everyone feels able to step in.

  2. 2

    Set the scene

    A real situation from your workplace, with characters the room recognises.

  3. 3

    Play it out

    Volunteers step into the scene. The rest of the room watches closely.

  4. 4

    Freeze and reflect

    We pause, swap roles and ask what each person is thinking and needs.

  5. 5

    Replay and take it back

    Rewind, try a new approach, and leave with the words you will use.

A conflict, from start to end

One scene, three takes

A missed deadline between a team lead and an analyst, the way we would run it in a session. Scroll through to watch it change.

Meera, team leadArjun, analystFacilitatorThe room

Act 1The scene, as it usually goes

Meera · team lead

“Arjun, the client report was due yesterday. Again. What happened?”

Arjun · analyst

“I told you last week the data wasn’t ready. Nobody listened.”

Meera · team lead

“I don’t need excuses. I need it done.”

Arjun · analyst

“Fine. I’ll stay late. Like always.”

Arjun goes quiet. The conversation is over, and nothing is solved.

FreezeThe facilitator stops the scene

Freeze

Everyone holds still. “What is Arjun thinking right now? And what does Meera need from him?”

The room

“He feels blamed for something he flagged.” · “She is under pressure from the client.”

Act 2Swap roles

Swap

“Meera, you are Arjun now. Arjun, you play Meera. Same scene.”

Meera · team lead

(as Arjun) “I flagged this. If I push back again, I look difficult. So I just say yes.”

Meera · team lead

(stepping out of role) “I didn’t know he had flagged it. I was reacting to the client, not to him.”

Act 3Rewind and replay

Rewind

“Same moment, new approach. Start with curiosity before judgement.”

Meera · team lead

“Arjun, the report was late and the client noticed. Before I jump in, what got in the way?”

Arjun · analyst

“The data came in on Thursday. I flagged it, but I should have chased harder.”

Meera · team lead

“That helps. What would stop this happening next time?”

Arjun · analyst

“A check-in two days before each deadline, so we spot gaps early.”

Meera · team lead

“Let’s do that. I’ll give the client a new date today.”

ReflectWhat changed, and what you take back to work

Debrief

The room names what changed: a question instead of a verdict, facts instead of labels, one agreed next step. Then everyone writes the opening line they will use in their next one-to-one.

Questions

Good to know

Do I need acting experience?

No. This is not about performing. Most people who join have never been on a stage, and the games start small and safe.

Do I need a degree or a training background?

No. The workshop and the certification are open to adults from any field. For the 3-day certification, it helps to have a group you can practise with between the days, and we can help you find one.

What do I receive?

The 1-day workshop gives a certificate of participation. The 3-day certification gives an Unscripted certificate in applied theatre facilitation, once you pass the practicum. Both are professional training certificates from Unscripted, not an academic degree or diploma.

Where and when does it run?

We confirm the dates, city and format with each group. Request fee details after you get the brochure, and we will send the next options.

How much does it cost?

Fees depend on the format and group size. Register for the brochure below, then request fee details and we will email you fees, dates and city.

Can you run it for my team?

Yes. We write the scenes from your own workplace and run it in one or two days, with follow-up afterwards.
