How pulse surveys work

A short, regular check on how people really feel at work

A pulse survey is a quick, anonymous questionnaire, usually 5 to 15 questions, sent to employees at regular intervals. Unlike a long annual survey, it shows how things are changing, team by team, while there is still time to act.

Engagement

Pride, motivation, and whether people would recommend the company as a place to work (often reported as eNPS, a score from −100 to +100).

Manager effectiveness

Recognition, feedback, support, trust, and fairness: how people experience their own manager.

Wellbeing and workload

Stress, balance, and whether the workload is manageable.

Belonging and growth

Inclusion, speaking up safely, career growth, and intent to stay.

The questions

Short statements rated on a 1 to 5 agreement scale, such as “My manager gives me useful feedback”, plus one or two open comment boxes.

The results

Shown as the share of favourable answers per question, compared with the last pulse and broken down by team, usually as a heatmap. Groups under about five people are hidden to protect anonymity.

The gap

Most organisations collect the data well. Far fewer turn it into action, and when nothing changes, people stop answering honestly. That is where we come in.

Times when it matters most

Scores have dropped in one or more teams since the last pulse.

After a restructure, merger or leadership change, when trust is fragile.

During fast growth, when many people have been promoted into their first manager role.

When attrition is rising and exit interviews point to managers.

Before an annual engagement survey, to fix known issues while there is time.

When comments mention burnout, favouritism, or not feeling heard.

Cadence

How often to run a pulse

There is no single right answer. The best cadence is the one you can act on before the next survey arrives.

Monthly

During big change

For restructures, integrations or fast-moving teams. Keep it to 3 to 5 questions so people do not tire of it.

Quarterly · Most common

The steady rhythm

Long enough to act, short enough to see change. It lines up with our 30, 60 and 90-day follow-up, so each pulse measures our impact.

Twice a year

For stable teams

Works for smaller or settled organisations, alongside regular manager one-to-ones.

Annual + pulses

The full picture

A longer annual engagement survey for depth, with short pulses in between to track progress.

Mitigation for hotspots

When a team needs more than a workshop

Some results need a closer, quieter response. These sit alongside the workshops, led by facilitators with clinical psychology and coaching backgrounds.

One-to-one manager coaching

Confidential coaching for managers whose teams scored lowest, focused on the specific themes their team raised.

Facilitated listening sessions

A neutral facilitator meets the team without the manager present, to understand the comments behind the scores.

Manager action plans

A simple plan for each manager: three commitments, how they will share them with the team, and when they will check in.

Confidentiality

Your people’s trust is the point. We protect it.

Pulse surveys only work if employees believe their answers are safe. Everything we do keeps it that way.

  • Aggregated reports only. We never ask for or receive individual responses.
  • Groups of five or more. We do not report on any group small enough to identify someone.
  • A mutual NDA before you share anything.
  • Handled under India’s data protection law, stored securely and deleted when the engagement ends.
  • Comments are themed, never quoted in a way that could identify the person who wrote them.

Questions

Common questions

We don’t run a pulse survey yet. Can you help?

Yes. We can run a short anonymous pulse for you, 8 to 12 questions built around your values, and use it as the baseline before training.

Our response rate was low. Is the report still useful?

Usually, yes. We look at which teams answered and which did not; a low response rate can itself be a signal about trust. We will tell you honestly what the data can and cannot support.

Will managers feel blamed?

No. Workshops are framed around team themes, not individual scores. Managers who need extra support get it privately through coaching.

How do we know the workshops worked?

Your next pulse is the measure. We compare the same questions for the same teams, and our free check-ins at 30, 60 or 90 days fill the gap in between.

Which survey tools do you work with?

Any. We only need the aggregated report: scores by question and team, trends, and themed comments. Most tools export this as a PDF or spreadsheet.
